Education


Tyler School of Art, TempleUniversity, Elkins Park, PA 19126

  • MFA in Metals/CAD-CAM (1993-1996)

Pursued the development of CAD/CAM processes for the creation of craft objects in the computer environment. Focused on designing functional objects that express both traditional and contemporary elements of form and concept. Researched the means by which virtual objects existing in computer space could be realized as tangible objects in real space through the use of a 3-axis CNC milling machine.

Worked toward the development of an aesthetic true to my personal evolution and appropriate to the computer environment. Created both holloware and jewelry objects that embody my heritage as a metalsmith/jeweler and respond to my participation as an artist in the electronic age.



University of Washington, Seattle, WA 98195

  • Post Baccalaureate, (1992-1993)

Attended the University as a fifth year student in the Metal Design Program. Concentrated on traditional smithing skills for creating hollowware. Further developed and refined my knowledge of traditional jewelry skills. My formal metals training prior to this time came from my involvement in the marketplace as a jewelry designer. My purpose in attending the university as a fifth year student was to prepare myself for graduate level study in jewelry and metal design.



Rhode Island School of Design, Two College Street, Providence, RI 02903

  • Graduate, Non-Matriculating, (1991-1992)

Returned to school to pursue my MFA in metal/jewelry design. Attended R.I.S.D. as a non-matriculating student in the graduate department.



Grinnell College, P.O. Box 805, Grinnell, IA 50112

  • BA-Philosophy, (1981-1984)

Studied philosophies of art, language, law, science and religion. The strongest concentration of my studies was in the philosophy of art which culminated in an independent study of the concept of beauty in 20th century art movements, focusing on the Dadaists.




Experience


Tyler School of Art

  • Teaching Metalsmithing, (Spring 1995)

Taught metalsmithing and hollowware to advanced undergraduate metals majors. The students were taught traditional raising, forming and large scale fabrication techniques. Demonstrations of advanced skills such as spinning and shell forming were given mainly for the students in their second semester of metalsmithing who were encouraged to work independently.



Tyler School of Art

  • Technical Assistantship, (Fall 1994)

Maintained an extensive tool and supply inventory for the studio. Supervised a six person staff of tool room monitors. Designed and constructed new tool storage facilities. Maintained a check out system for tools and a sales record for supplies.



Tyler School of Art

  • Teaching-Beginning Jewelry, (1993-1996)

Taught beginning jewelry for the Saturday Program. This program offered classes to the community, attracting prospective undergraduate students from local high schools as well as art teachers, hobbyists and others. The fundamentals of fabrication and lost wax casting of metals were covered in addition to an introduction to forming and fabricating with plastics.



Ag 47 Studio/Gallery, 163 South Jackson Street, Seattle WA 98104

  • Design and Production, (1992-1993)

Performed fabrication and casting for limited edition production line of men's and women's jewelry. In addition a separate case in the gallery was dedicated to my personal work.



Faces of Time, 32 West 40th Street, New York, NY 10018

  • Design and Production, (1987-1992)

Head Jewelry Designer. Supervised a staff of four craftsmen. Designed and created single edition signed works. Developed prototypes for antique reproduction product lines. Repaired and reconstructed antique jewelry, hollowware and furniture.
